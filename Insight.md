# Insights & Recommendations

## 1. Attrition is high but evenly distributed
The company has an overall attrition rate of **31%** — significantly above the typical industry benchmark of 10–15%. However, the stacked bar chart shows that the Active / Inactive / Pending split is remarkably consistent across all six departments (DevOps, Sales, HR, Finance, Admin, Cloud Tech).

**Recommendation:** This is a company-wide retention problem, not a departmental one. HR should investigate exit-interview themes that apply across the whole organization — compensation, career progression, or management quality.

## 2. Compensation is unusually flat
The average salary across the entire company is **$85.16K**, and the average is nearly identical across every department and every region (California, Florida, Illinois, Nevada, New York, Texas). Excellent performers earn roughly the same as poor performers.

**Recommendation:** The company appears to use a highly standardized pay band. This is good for internal equity but may cause problems in high-cost regions like California and New York, where competitors likely pay more. Consider adding regional cost-of-labor adjustments and performance-linked bonuses.

## 3. Remote work does not hurt performance
50% of employees work remotely (513 of 1,020). The performance distribution — Good (~270), Average (~267), Excellent (~267), Poor (~216) — is almost perfectly split between remote and on-site staff.

**Recommendation:** The company can safely expand remote work eligibility without fear of performance drops. There is no evidence in this data that remote work correlates with lower performance.

## 4. Hiring has remained stable
The line chart shows the company hires roughly 200 new employees per year, with a slight dip in 2021, a peak in 2023, and a slight pullback in 2024.

**Recommendation:** Hiring is healthy but not accelerating. If headcount growth is a goal, the company needs to ramp up recruitment efforts in 2025.
